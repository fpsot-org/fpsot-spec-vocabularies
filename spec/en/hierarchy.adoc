= Hierarchy and collections
include::_fpsot-terms.adoc[]

A scheme can arrange its concepts in a tree, from broad to narrow.
It can also group concepts for a screen or a report without changing the tree.

== Hierarchy

[[VOC-020]]
*VOC-020.* Each concept without a broader concept MUST be a top concept of its scheme.
The file MUST state both `skos:topConceptOf` on the concept and `skos:hasTopConcept` on the scheme.
A tool then finds the entry points of the tree without a search through every concept.

[[VOC-021]]
*VOC-021.* A hierarchy MUST use `skos:broader` and `skos:narrower`, and the published files MUST state both directions.
These links MUST stay within one scheme.
A link to a concept of another scheme is a mapping, under VOC-041.
The link:https://www.w3.org/TR/skos-reference/[SKOS Reference] defines each property as the inverse of the other.
A tool without a reasoner still sees both directions when the file states them.

[[VOC-022]]
*VOC-022.* A chain of broader links MUST NOT return to the concept where it started.
A concept SHOULD have at most one broader concept.
A concept MAY have two or more when it truly belongs under each of them.
The {abbr-skos-first} model allows cycles, but code lists that drive forms and reports need a tree.
A thesaurus under link:https://www.iso.org/standard/53657.html[ISO 25964-1] may have several broader terms, and some vocabularies need that too.

[[VOC-023]]
*VOC-023.* In a broader link, the narrower concept MUST be a kind, part or instance of the broader one.
Any other link between two concepts of one scheme MUST use `skos:related`.
A related link MUST NOT join two concepts on the same chain of broader links.
Readers and tools expect everything under a concept to share its meaning.
The last condition is one of the integrity conditions of {abbr-skos}.

== Collections

[[VOC-024]]
*VOC-024.* A group of concepts that is not itself a concept MUST be a `skos:Collection`, with labels under VOC-010.
A group whose order matters MUST be a `skos:OrderedCollection`.
Data MUST NOT store a collection as a code.
An example is the group of documents that a business can request.
A collection changes what a screen shows, but it does not change the hierarchy or the meaning of a code.

[[VOC-025]]
*VOC-025.* The order in which a list shows concepts MUST come from an ordered collection or from the labels.
Labels sort with the rules of the language of the reader, under LOC-015.
The order MUST NOT come from the codes.
Codes are in English, so their order makes no sense to a French reader.
